# Harbourline Bank — Vulnerable Customers Policy (VUL)
*Fictional policy written for a prototype. Version 3.2, effective 1 April 2026. Owner: Customer Vulnerability Lead.*

**VUL-1 Definition.** A vulnerable customer is someone who, due to their personal circumstances, is especially susceptible to harm. The four drivers are: *health* (physical or mental health conditions, including cognitive decline), *life events* (bereavement, job loss, relationship breakdown, caring responsibilities), *resilience* (low or erratic income, problem debt, financial difficulty) and *capability* (low literacy, low digital skills, limited English, or a lack of experience with financial products).

**VUL-2 Identify.** Handlers must look for indicators of vulnerability in everything the customer says or writes, including indirect statements (for example "since my husband passed I've been struggling with all of this"). If in doubt, record it as a possible vulnerability and ask the customer, sensitively, whether they need any support.

**VUL-3 Record.** Indicators must be recorded in the case with the customer's consent, using the four drivers. The exact health details should only be recorded where needed to support the customer.

**VUL-4 Refer.** Cases with a vulnerability indicator *and* any of: financial difficulty, risk to health or wellbeing, suspected financial abuse, or bereavement must be referred to the Vulnerable Customer Support team.

**VUL-5 Adjust.** Reasonable adjustments must be offered and recorded, for example a preferred contact method, a named contact, more time to respond, or communication with a nominated third party with the customer's authority.

**VUL-6 Third parties.** The bank may only discuss the account with a third party (such as a relative or carer) where there is a valid authority on file (third-party mandate or power of attorney).
